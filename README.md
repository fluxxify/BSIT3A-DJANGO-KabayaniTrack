## The Problem and Its Background

### Introduction

Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs) play an important role in supporting their families and contributing to the Philippine economy. However, OFWs may experience difficulties while working abroad, such as problems with their employment, welfare, safety, and access to reliable support information. When they need assistance, it may be challenging to find the appropriate government agencies, contact details, and available support services. Monitoring the situations and concerns of OFWs can also be difficult when information is scattered across different sources.

Currently, accessing information about OFWs and their concerns may require searching through different websites, contacting various offices, or relying on social media posts. This can make it difficult for OFWs, their families, and authorized personnel to find updated and reliable information quickly. Without a centralized system, monitoring relevant information and identifying the appropriate support services may become less organized and less convenient.

To address these concerns, the researchers propose KabayaniTrack: An OFW Monitoring and Support Information System. The system focuses on providing a centralized platform for monitoring OFW-related information and accessing important support resources. It aims to organize relevant information, provide access to emergency hotlines and government agency contacts, and display registered locations through an interactive map when applicable. These features can help users locate relevant information more conveniently and identify appropriate channels for assistance.

The development of KabayaniTrack is necessary because an Information Technology solution can make OFW-related information more accessible, organized, and easier to monitor. By providing a centralized source of monitoring information and support resources, the system aims to help OFWs and their families become better informed and make it easier to find appropriate assistance when needed. The system is intended to support information access and monitoring, rather than replace the official services and responsibilities of government agencies.

### Objectives of the Study

The general objective of the study is to develop KabayaniTrack: An OFW Monitoring and Support Information System that provides a centralized platform for monitoring OFW-related information and accessing essential support resources. The system aims to make information more organized, accessible, and convenient for OFWs, their families, and authorized users.

**Specific Objectives**

Specifically, this study aims to:

- Develop an OFW Monitoring System that organizes and displays relevant information about registered OFWs.
- Provide Support Information by displaying important government agency contacts, emergency hotlines, and available support resources for OFWs.
- Integrate an Interactive Map that allows users to view registered locations relevant to OFW monitoring.
- Improve Information Accessibility by providing a centralized platform where users can easily find relevant OFW information and support services.
- Organize OFW-Related Information to make monitoring and retrieving available records more convenient for authorized users.
- Evaluate the System's Usability and Functionality to determine whether KabayaniTrack meets its intended objectives and provides a convenient experience for its users.

## Scope of the Study

### 1.1 Project Setting & Target User Groups

KabayaniTrack is designed as a specialized web and mobile-responsive information system intended for deployment within foreign labor monitoring offices, embassy welfare desks, and designated Philippine government welfare channels (such as DMW and OWWA). The system connects three distinct user groups:

- **Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs):** Registered migrant workers accessing the mobile client interface to update location status, submit distress/help requests, and access official guidance.
- **OFW Families / Beneficiaries:** Authorized family members residing in the Philippines who can view status updates and verify welfare reports during emergencies.
- **System Administrators & Welfare Officers:** Government or administrative personnel managing the centralized dashboard, processing incident tickets, tracking worker demographics, and issuing emergency announcements.

### 1.2 Core Functional Modules

The scope of KabayaniTrack covers four primary functional modules designed to streamline worker monitoring and assistance:

- **Centralized Worker Profile & Location Registry:** Digitizes worker demographic records, employment details, host country location, contract duration, and emergency contact details.
- **Emergency Alert & Welfare Ticket Management:** Enables OFWs to trigger SOS/distress alerts and lodge welfare assistance requests, which generate prioritized administrative tickets for desk officers.
- **Administrative Analytics & Status Dashboard:** Provides real-time spatial and demographic mapping of registered OFWs, incident tracking metrics, and exportable reports for decision-makers.
- **Resource & Advisory Broadcast Hub:** Serves as an official communications channel to publish embassy advisories, emergency protocols, and consular contact information.

### 1.3 Technical Architecture & Technology Stack

KabayaniTrack follows a modern 3-tier architecture (Presentation, Application, and Database Layer) designed for security, responsiveness, and scalability:

| Layer / Component | Technology / Framework | System Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frontend (User Web/Mobile) | React.js / Tailwind CSS / PWA | Responsive user interface for OFWs, families, and web admin portal. |
| Backend Services | Node.js (Express) / Python (FastAPI) | RESTful API handling ticketing logic, user authentication, and data requests. |
| Database Layer | PostgreSQL / MongoDB | Secure relational storage for user profiles, incident logs, and system audit trails. |
| Authentication & Security | JWT / HTTPS / AES-256 Encryption | Ensures role-based access control and encryption of sensitive personal data. |
| Mapping & Communications | Leaflet API / Twilio SMS Service | Geographic data rendering and automated SMS notifications during emergencies. |

## Limitations and Delimitations of the Study

To establish realistic boundaries for KabayaniTrack, the system distinguishes between delimitations (intentional choices made by the researchers to narrow the scope) and limitations (external factors beyond the researchers' control).

### 2.1 System Delimitations (Scope Boundaries)

- **Exclusion of Financial & Remittance Processing:** The system explicitly excludes financial features, such as bank transfers, remittance tracking, loan applications, or OWWA/SS/Pag-IBIG contribution payment processing.
- **Exclusion of Hardware GPS Tracking:** The system does not utilize active, real-time hardware GPS tracking devices or passive background location monitoring. Location updates are user-initiated via check-ins or manual status submissions.
- **Exclusion of Direct Legal Representation:** The platform functions solely as an information management and dispatch platform; it does not provide legal advisory tools or automated legal counsel features.

### 2.2 System Limitations (External Constraints)

- **Dependence on Host Country Telecommunication & Internet Infrastructure:** Real-time dispatch of SOS alerts, location check-ins, and ticket submissions relies heavily on active internet connections or cellular mobile coverage in host countries.
- **Data Accuracy & User Integrity:** The accuracy of demographic records and status updates relies on the truthfulness and timeliness of self-reported data submitted by OFWs and verification approvals by administrators.
- **Third-Party Service Dependencies:** Features such as automated SMS notifications and interactive geographical map displays are subject to third-party API availability, service quotas, and network gateway performance.
- **Operational Jurisdiction Constraints:** Physical rescue operations, emergency extractions, and resolution of labor disputes remain entirely dependent on government agency responsiveness and host country laws.
